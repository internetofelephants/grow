Information & guide

Watch trees grow

Choose some trees, press play, and watch them grow side by side from seed. Every second is a year of their lives.

How to use it

  • Pick 1 to 5 trees from the buttons at the top. The second row has trees from other parts of the world.
  • Press ▶ play. Each second is one year. Use 1×, 5× or 20× to speed time up; some trees live for thousands of years.
  • Pause at any time, or drag the slider to move through the years.
  • Reset clears the trees back to seed. Your trees stay chosen, and you can pick different ones. (Trees can't be changed while they're growing, so nothing gives away how big a tree gets.)
  • Let trees die: leave it ticked to see each tree die at the end of a typical lifespan. Untick it to keep every tree alive, so you can compare them all at full size; a note shows when each one would have died.

Reading the picture

  • All the trees are drawn to the same scale, in metres. The little figure by the axis is a person, 1.75 m tall.
  • Under each tree is its name, its scientific name and its height. "Full size" means it has nearly finished growing taller.
  • The heights, growth and lifespans are typical values from forestry research. Real trees vary a lot with soil, climate and luck. The sources are in References.

Keys

  • Space: play or pause
  • ?: open or close this panel · Esc: close it

The book “The Wild Trees” by Richard Preston is what originally opened my eyes to the implications of how long a redwood tree lives. Many of those still standing today would have been alive during the Middle Ages, and what really got me were all the other lichens and mosses that can only live when one of these trees reaches a certain height at hundreds of years old. Which means that if one of them is cut down or burns, it will be hundreds of years before those organisms can grow again. A bristlecone pine tree can live for thousands of years while reaching measly heights. Baobabs have trunks that can reach 100 feet around. Trees can live in the most urban of cities, the driest of deserts, and in some of the coldest places on earth. They are dogged in their pursuit of growth and proliferation. We take trees for granted.

I thought it would be interesting to see, relative to each other, how trees grow in terms of how long it takes and also how high and wide they get. I’m not a botanist and so the representation here is probably not 100% accurate, but it’s also probably directionally correct, even when accounting for creative license to make a pleasing experience. I’ve included the spreadsheet of data that was used to model the simulation in the References in case you want to fact check.

It should be pretty obvious that Grow was built almost entirely by AI. Of course, there are many things we would love to have had the help of a botanist, an illustrator, a creative director, and a game developer to get exactly right. But what’s exciting is that we didn’t need a whole team of people to get started. We had an idea, some curiosity, a couple of computers and a self-imposed timeline of less than a week. So that’s what we did. And we’re pretty excited about what else we might be able to make this way.

We hope you enjoy this and many more similar experiences to come.

Kalindi and Gautam
